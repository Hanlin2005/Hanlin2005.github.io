JuliaChess

Ongoing

Tools: Julia

A position from the engine. The highlighted squares on the d-file mark the last move.

I am writing a chess engine in Julia and playing it on Lichess. The board above is a position from that engine. A game on Lichess is the test I actually want: the program has to choose a move, send it, and keep doing that until the game ends.

The work sits in three pieces. The board has to remember where every piece is. Move generation has to list only legal moves, including castling, en passant, and promotion. Search has to look ahead far enough to pick a move before the clock runs out.

The search uses minimax and Monte Carlo tree search. The engine plays on Lichess through the Universal Chess Interface, and I benchmark search depth and heuristics under a time limit.
