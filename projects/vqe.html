Variational Quantum Eigensolver

Tools: Python, Qiskit

For ECE 520 I used the variational quantum eigensolver to estimate the ground state of a 4-qubit XYZ Heisenberg model. The circuit prepares a trial state, a classical optimizer updates the parameters, and the quantum part only estimates the energy. I ran it in Qiskit on the Aer simulator.

The question was what happens when the problem’s connectivity does not match the chip. I crossed a 1D and a 2D problem with a linear chain and a square grid. The ansatz was EfficientSU2, two layers of Ry rotations and CNOTs, with the entanglement laid out like the problem rather than like the chip. A missing link has to be routed with SWAP gates, and each SWAP is three CNOT gates. I put a depolarizing error of 0.03 on every CNOT, so each extra SWAP adds noise and nothing else does.

Without noise, the geometry barely mattered. With noise, a 2D problem on a linear chip was the costly case: the compiler inserted six SWAPs and the energy error was about twice the matched 1D run. A 1D problem on a square chip was not penalized, because the extra links went unused. SPSA stayed closer to the true energy. COBYLA converged in fewer steps and more often settled in a local minimum.
